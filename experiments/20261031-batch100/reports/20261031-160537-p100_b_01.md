## intraday_vs_overnight_volume_imbalance

**hypothesis**: Stocks whose overnight share of volume (overnight volume / total volume) is abnormally high over the past 20 days underperform stocks whose overnight share is abnormally low over the next 5 trading days.

**economic rationale**: The overnight session is dominated by retail and liquidity-motivated flow, while the intraday session absorbs informed institutional trading. When a stock's volume is unusually concentrated overnight relative to its own recent history, the marginal buyer is more likely to be uninformed, and the price move is more likely to revert. Conversely, intraday-heavy volume signals informed accumulation that persists. This is a flow-composition effect, distinct from a simple volume shock because it conditions on when in the day the volume occurs.

## close_location_persistence

**hypothesis**: Stocks with a high average close_position over the past 20 days outperform stocks with a low average close_position over the next 5 trading days.

**economic rationale**: The close position within the daily range (close_position) captures whether buyers or sellers won the end-of-day auction. When a stock consistently closes near its high over many days, it reflects persistent end-of-day demand from index funds, passive flows, or informed traders who prefer to execute at the close. This persistent pressure is not fully incorporated into price immediately and tends to continue over the following week. The mechanism is order-flow persistence at the close, not short-term reversal.

## range_to_close_volatility_ratio

**hypothesis**: Stocks with a high 20-day average of (range / |close-to-close return|) underperform stocks with a low average over the next 5 trading days.

**economic rationale**: The ratio of the daily high-low range to the absolute close-to-close return measures how much intraday price movement is 'wasted' (round-tripped) versus how much is a directional move. Stocks with persistently high range-to-return ratios are experiencing high intraday noise and low directional conviction, which attracts mean-reversion traders and deters trend followers. Such stocks tend to underperform because the noise premium is negative: investors demand compensation for holding volatile, unpredictable names. This is a volatility-of-volatility effect at the daily level.

## intraday_return_autocorrelation_20d

**economic rationale**: Intraday returns reflect the continuous trading process where informed traders gradually impound information. When intraday returns are positively autocorrelated over a 20-day window, it indicates that information is being incorporated slowly and predictably, allowing momentum traders to profit. When autocorrelation is negative, the market is efficient or dominated by mean-reversion liquidity providers. Stocks with high intraday return autocorrelation should therefore exhibit stronger short-term momentum.
