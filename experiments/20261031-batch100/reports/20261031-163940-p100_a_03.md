## overnight_intraday_return_divergence

**hypothesis**: Stocks whose 20-day cumulative overnight return exceeds their 20-day cumulative intraday return (overnight-minus-intraday spread) outperform over the next 5 trading days; the cross-sectional rank of the spread positively predicts forward returns.

**economic rationale**: Overnight and intraday returns are driven by different clienteles: overnight moves reflect information/news absorbed while markets are closed and are dominated by institutional and informed order flow, while intraday moves reflect retail liquidity provision and noise. When a stock's cumulative overnight return persistently diverges from its cumulative intraday return over a month, the overnight component carries the more durable information and tends to continue, whereas the intraday component mean-reverts. Investors are paid for holding the informed, news-driven leg because it is under-reacted to by slower traders.

Stocks with low amount-to-cap ratios are neglected, have wide effective spreads, and require compensation for illiquidity; stocks with very high ratios are attention-driven and prone to overpricing. The cross-sectional rank of amount-to-cap captures a liquidity/neglect premium that is distinct from size because it normalizes trading activity by the stock's own scale.
