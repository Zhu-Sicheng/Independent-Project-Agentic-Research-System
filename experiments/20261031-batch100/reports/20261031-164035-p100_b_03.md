## overnight_intraday_flow_drift

**hypothesis**: Stocks whose cumulative overnight returns exceed cumulative intraday returns over the trailing 20 trading days (overnight-minus-intraday spread, cross-sectionally ranked) outperform over the next 5 trading days.

**economic rationale**: The overnight session is dominated by informed and institutional order flow reacting to news and global cues, while the intraday session is increasingly dominated by retail and liquidity-providing market makers. When a stock persistently gaps up overnight but fades intraday, the informed overnight buyers are being systematically absorbed by liquidity suppliers; the accumulated overnight drift signals genuine fundamental repricing that has not yet fully propagated into the close. This creates a persistent, under-reacted information channel that pays patient capital.

A stock that repeatedly closes in the upper part of its range over weeks is being accumulated by buyers willing to pay up into the close, while persistent lower-range closes indicate distribution. ## turnover_volatility_ratio_premium

**hypothesis**: Stocks with a low 20-day average turnover-to-volatility ratio (volume relative to return dispersion) outperform stocks with a high ratio over the next 5 trading days.

**economic rationale**: The ratio of average daily turnover to return volatility measures how much trading activity is required to move the price. Low turnover relative to volatility indicates that a stock is priced efficiently with little disagreement (low attention, low speculative churn), while high turnover relative to volatility signals speculative excess and crowded positioning that subsequently mean-reverts. Stocks with low turnover-to-volatility ratios are neglected, efficiently priced names that command a premium as speculative froth unwinds in high-ratio names.

- **diagnosis**: No signal: the turnover-to-volatility ratio carries near-zero cross-sectional information (IC ~0), and the sign of the effect flips across regimes (positive IS, strongly negative OOS), so the mechanism is not robustly identified.
