## intraday_overnight_return_divergence_20d

**hypothesis**: Over a 5-day horizon, stocks with a high 20-day sum of overnight returns relative to their 20-day sum of intraday returns (overnight-led appreciation) underperform stocks where recent returns were intraday-led; the cross-sectional rank IC of the divergence measure on forward 5-day returns is negative.

**economic rationale**: A stock's total return decomposes into an overnight (close-to-open) and an intraday (open-to-close) component, and these two are driven by different clienteles: overnight moves often reflect news, retail sentiment and gap-driven flow, while intraday moves reflect institutional execution and liquidity provision. When a stock's cumulative overnight return persistently outweighs its intraday return, the gap move is being 'paid for' by overnight buyers who are later met by intraday sellers unwinding, so the divergence tends to mean-revert. Stocks whose recent gains came mostly overnight are more over-extended than those whose gains came intraday, so the former should underperform over the following weeks.

A stock whose 20-day cumulative return is dominated by overnight moves has been rewarded mainly for overnight gap exposure, and the marginal overnight buyer is more likely a short-horizon sentiment trader who will exit, pushing price back. Stocks whose returns came intraday reflect more durable institutional accumulation. Thus overnight-dominated gainers should reverse relative to intraday-dominated gainers.
