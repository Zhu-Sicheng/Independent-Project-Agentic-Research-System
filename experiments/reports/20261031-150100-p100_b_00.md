Stocks whose recent returns were dominated by the overnight session (a large positive overnight-minus-intraday spread) tend to be pushed by transient, non-informational demand that reverses over the following week. Range contraction often precedes directional resolution, and stocks in tight ranges are systematically under-owned by volatility sellers, so they earn a premium as the range re-expands. This is a volatility-timing effect distinct from price momentum.
